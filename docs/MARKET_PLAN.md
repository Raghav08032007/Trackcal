# TrackCal: competitors and market plan

Researched November 2026. Competitor prices come from their own pages where possible. Figures marked *unverified* come from third-party sites and must be checked in the live apps before being quoted publicly.

## In one paragraph

TrackCal is the only app in this comparison that combines all four of these:

- Indian food logging that understands katoris, rotis and cooked dishes;
- a camera coach that counts reps on the phone and says what to fix;
- full use in 16 languages;
- a free core.

Competitors each own one corner: HealthifyMe (Indian food plus paid human coaches), MyFitnessPal (the biggest food database, barcode behind a paywall), cult.fit (classes and gyms) and camera-coach apps (form feedback, English only and fitness only). The opening is **"the coach in your pocket, in your language, for Indian food and home workouts, at a tenth of the price"**. The biggest risk is proving the camera coach on real people before saying so loudly.

## The market

- **India fitness apps:** about US$436–521 million in 2024–25, growing 14–19% a year, depending on the report ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-fitness-app-market), [IBEF](https://www.ibef.org/blogs/how-fitness-apps-are-reshaping-health-and-wellness-habits-in-india), [6Wresearch](https://www.6wresearch.com/market-takeaways-view/what-is-the-size-of-digital-fitness-market-in-india)). The reports define the market differently. Use the range, not one number.
- **Smartphone base:** over 900 million active smartphone users in India (IMARC). Most of them are more comfortable in an Indian language than in English.
- **Funding:** investors have pulled back from fitness tech ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/in-depth/sweat-tech-and-a-funding-freeze-indias-fitness-tech-isnt-slowing-down-even-after-capital-crunch)). Low running costs (on-device AI, no human coaches needed) are an advantage.

## Where TrackCal wins today

1. **Indian food that's logged right.**
   - 542 IFCT foods plus 92 cooked dishes, with katori and roti portions and local names.
   - Typing "rice" finds cooked rice, not raw grain.
   - A 34-food benchmark scores 100/100 on the right food, calories and portion.
2. **A camera coach that explains.**
   - Counts reps on the device, with no video uploaded.
   - Tells you why a rep didn't count ("you went 70% of the way"), the camera angle to use, and the form fix, out loud.
   - A 3D demo that can look like you.
3. **16 languages,** including 11 Indian ones, plus Arabic, Indonesian, Spanish, Portuguese, French and German.
4. **A free core.** Logging, barcode, camera workouts and games are free. Premium (₹99/month, ₹799/year) adds long history and extras.
5. **Privacy as a feature.** Camera and photo processing stay on the device, which suits the DPDP Act and wary users.
6. **Fun.** Your character, levels, sports games and challenges, which most nutrition apps don't have.

## Where TrackCal is behind (fix or don't promise)

| Gap | What to do |
|---|---|
| Camera accuracy not yet measured on real people (the video bench is ready) | 50–100 volunteer videos before launch; publish the per-exercise accuracy. Keep "Experimental" labels until each exercise reaches ±1 rep on 90% of sets |
| No human coaches (the coach tier exists, priced at ₹499/month as a placeholder) | Launch without it. Add coaches through partner gyms and dietitians later |
| A web app (PWA), not in the Play Store or App Store | Package for Play Store first (TWA), which is where most Indian users install apps. iOS later |
| Brand trust is zero | Dietitian review of the recipes; ICMR-NIN permission for the IFCT data; published accuracy numbers; real user stories |
| Food database outside India is small | Run the full USDA import before marketing abroad. Lead with India |
| AI photo estimates cost money per photo | Keep photo scanning in a fair-use allowance; search, barcode and voice stay unlimited |

## Who to win first

1. **Home exercisers, 18–35, in Tier 1–3 cities,** who would rather use Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi or another Indian language. They can't afford ₹5,000+ coaching, and video-class apps don't check their form.
2. **Students and young professionals** counting calories with Indian food. MFP gets their dal and roti wrong, and HealthifyMe's paid tiers are out of reach.
3. **Later:** gyms and colleges (group challenges, the coach tier), and corporate wellness (team challenges and anonymous summaries).

## Pricing recommendation

- **Keep the core free.** Logging, barcode, camera counting with the live coach, games and your character. This is the main weapon against MyFitnessPal's barcode paywall and HealthifyMe's price.
- **Premium at ₹99/month or ₹799/year** (as planned). Test ₹599 for the first year as a launch offer, with a 7-day trial and cancelling as easy as subscribing (already built). Fill Premium with things that cost money or add depth:
  - unlimited AI photo meals;
  - full history;
  - advanced programmes;
  - saving form videos;
  - a family plan for up to 4 people at ₹1,199/yr.
- **Coach tier: launch later,** only with real partner coaches. Price at ₹499–999/month, still below HealthifyMe's coach plans.
- **Abroad:** price by purchasing power, e.g. US$2.99/month or $19.99/year in Indonesia and Latin America, and $4.99/$29.99 in the US and EU. Still well below MFP's $79.99.

## Go-to-market, in four steps

| When | Step | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–6 | **Closed beta.** 100–200 people from colleges and gyms in 2–3 cities, in 3 languages (Hindi, Tamil, English) | Measure camera accuracy on real videos, D7 retention and the food searches that fail |
| Weeks 7–12 | **India launch.** Play Store listing in 11 Indian languages | 10,000 installs; 25% D7 retention; under 1% crash rate |
| Months 4–6 | **Grow with creators.** Regional-language fitness creators run 30-day challenges inside the app; WhatsApp sharing of streaks and levels | Shares per active user; invites that join |
| Months 6–12 | **Partners and abroad.** Gyms, colleges and corporate challenges; then Indonesia and Arabic-speaking markets, where the languages are already done | First B2B contracts; 2–4% paid conversion |

**Channels that fit the product:**
- Short videos of the camera coach correcting a squat in Tamil or Hindi. This is the most "show, don't tell" feature.
- Before/after food logs of everyday Indian meals.
- The free barcode scan as a direct answer to MFP's paywall.

## Risks

- **IFCT data permission** from ICMR-NIN is a launch gate (docs/DATA_SOURCES.md).
- **Health claims.** Keep "estimates, not medical advice" wording everywhere. No weight-loss promises. Under-18 rules for the feed.
- **Data protection.** India's DPDP Act 2023 covers consent, deletion and a grievance officer. Erasure and data minimisation are already built; the grievance contact and privacy notice need a legal review.
- **AI cost** per photo meal: keep allowances and caching.
- **Big players copying camera coaching.** Speed and language depth are the moat; publishing accuracy builds trust they can't copy overnight.
